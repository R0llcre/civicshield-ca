# Credential Verification (CA Immigration Help)

This knowledge base file is for **general information only** and is **not legal advice**.

## 1) What “accredited representative” and “recognized organization” mean

**What it is**
- In U.S. immigration matters, some non-lawyers may be allowed to represent clients if they are an **accredited representative** working with a **recognized organization** under EOIR's Recognition & Accreditation (R&A) program.
- Someone claiming they can represent you should be able to show up on the official EOIR/DOJ R&A rosters (or be a licensed attorney).

**What to do next**
- If a person says they are “accredited,” verify them on the official roster (name + organization).
- If a person says they are a “California attorney,” verify their license status on the California State Bar site.

**Sources**
- https://www.justice.gov/eoir/recognition-and-accreditation-program

## 2) How to verify an accredited representative (step-by-step)

**What it is**
- The safest approach is to verify **two things**:
  1) the person is listed as an accredited representative, and
  2) the organization is listed as a recognized organization.

**What to do next**
- Search the official R&A roster for the person’s name (and common variations).
- Confirm the organization name matches what the person told you (not just “similar”).
- Confirm the city/state match what you were told.
- If anything does not match, treat it as a warning sign and seek a second opinion.

**Sources**
- https://www.justice.gov/eoir/recognition-and-accreditation-roster-reports

## 3) How to verify a California attorney (State Bar)

**What it is**
- If someone claims to be an attorney in California, you should check that they:
  - appear in the State Bar attorney search, and
  - have an active/eligible status for practice (status wording may vary).

**What to do next**
- Use the California State Bar attorney search.
- Check the attorney’s name and bar number (if provided).
- Prefer using the official profile page, not screenshots someone sent you.

**Sources**
- https://www.calbar.ca.gov/

## 4) How to verify an “immigration consultant” (CA SOS)

**What it is**
- In California, “immigration consultants” are regulated differently than attorneys and accredited representatives.
- Someone claiming to be a registered immigration consultant should be verifiable through California Secretary of State (SOS) resources.

**What to do next**
- Use the CA SOS consultant search.
- Confirm the name/business matches what you were given.
- If the person pressures you to skip verification, treat it as a red flag.

**Sources**
- https://www.sos.ca.gov/

## 5) Common verification red flags

**What it is**
- Scammers often use “near matches” (similar names, copied logos, fake emails) to appear legitimate.

**What to do next**
- If they refuse to share their full name, organization, or official profile links: pause.
- If they demand payment via gift cards/crypto/wire with urgency: pause.
- If they tell you not to contact official agencies or legal aid: pause.

**Sources**
- https://www.justice.gov/
