# Scam Safety (Immigration Help) — Practical Next Steps

This knowledge base file is for **general information only** and is **not legal advice**.

## 1) Common scam patterns

**What it is**
- Scammers often impersonate legal professionals, promise guaranteed outcomes, and use urgency to pressure payments.

**What to do next**
- Verify credentials using official sources before paying or signing anything.
- Be cautious with anyone asking for unusual payment methods (gift cards/crypto) or “today only” deadlines.
- If something feels off, pause and seek a second opinion from a verified source.

**Sources**
- https://www.justice.gov/

## 2) Safer communication and record-keeping

**What it is**
- Keeping clear records can help you explain what happened to a trusted organization later.

**What to do next**
- Keep copies of:
  - receipts and payment records
  - contracts or written agreements
  - screenshots of messages (with timestamps)
- Avoid sharing sensitive identifiers unless you are using a verified, official channel.

**Sources**
- https://www.uscis.gov/

## 3) Quick “stop and verify” checklist

**What it is**
- A short checklist to slow down high-pressure situations.

**What to do next**
- Ask for:
  - full name
  - organization name
  - official profile link(s) for verification
- Independently open the official site (don’t click a link they sent if you’re unsure) and verify.

**Sources**
- https://www.justice.gov/eoir/recognition-and-accreditation-program
- https://www.calbar.ca.gov/
- https://www.sos.ca.gov/
